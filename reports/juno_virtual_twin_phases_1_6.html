Juno Vale: a virtual twin's footage library, and what routing it buys

A fully virtual presenter — one gpt-image photo → one Seedance anchor clip → 8 emotion clips generated against that anchor as reference_video. Phase 1: identity (ArcFace 0.80–0.86), voice (0.92–0.96) and scripts (≥0.97) locked across the library. Phase 2: routing the matched emotion clip beats a neutral reference 7–1 on pairwise authenticity on fresh scripts. Phase 3, on 8 real production prompts: routed wins 5–3 with zero prompt-adherence cost — routing pays on expressive beats, the calm anchor is the right default for restrained ones, and identity survives every presenter-style scene. Phase 4: a fixed reference is itself the consistency mechanism — the library buys expressive range in her voice, not cohesion. Phase 5: routing wins the character axis 6–2 at the extremes, but Seedance undershoots the extremes in BOTH arms — an engine ceiling, not a footage problem. The recap below re-reads all five phases against the now-closed Aug 5–20 block; Phase 6 is the lineup test that would show whether the expressive range is actually hers.

caveat — read before quoting a margin

Juno's judge never had a control it could fail

  • The block voided two judges on exactly this ground: a VLM authenticity-to-self score rated real webcam footage 0.7–2.7/6, and the replay channel-fit judge ranked the creator's actual video 1st on 28% / 37% against a 33% chance line.
  • Juno's instrument is different — pairwise preference with the library studied first, not an absolute "moves like herself" score — and her identity, voice and transcript numbers are deterministic. Those are unaffected.
  • But 7–1 / 5–3 / 6–2 were never checked against a control the judge could fail. She is fully virtual, so a hidden-real-footage control is impossible by construction; the buildable analogue is a hidden library clip — drop one of her actual takes into the comparison and require the judge to pick it. Phase 6 builds that in.
  • Independently: routing costs identity where it changes the pick (−0.093 paired ArcFace on 7/9 newly-routed steps in the replay work) — consistent with Juno's routed-vs-control gap. Gate the routed reference on identity.

Why 3 gestures stay approximate — controlled diagnostic

Two hypotheses: (a) the calm anchor reference suppresses prompted gestures, or (b) count-precise hand choreography exceeds Seedance's text conditioning. We regenerated excited and serious with the photo as first_frame and no reference video:

Verdict: the engine, not the anchor. Removing the reference did NOT produce the double-clap or the fingertip steeple (both renders defaulted to hand-clasping) — but it DID cost −0.05–0.07 ArcFace and −0.09–0.11 voice cosine. The anchor-reference design is strictly better: it buys identity + voice at zero gesture cost. Count-precise choreography (double-clap, thumb-first counting, steepling) is below Seedance 2.0's text-conditioning resolution — the same class of miss as tokyo's triple air-chop null (5-arm grid, Jul 2026). Broad-stroke quirks (glasses push, lean-in, shrug, pointing, hands-to-head) transfer fine.

Cohesion hypothesis refuted — a fixed reference is itself the consistency mechanism. The control arm (same anchor every render) is as cohesive or marginally tighter on every uniformity metric. But the recurrence CONTENT reveals the real trade: control converges to ONE register in all four styles (hand-clasps at waist, glasses adjust, head tilt — the anchor's calm cluster, regardless of whether the script is excited or emphatic), while routed's recurring mannerisms differ by style and are her library signatures (hand-on-chest recurs in empathetic, finger-counting in emphatic). The muted within-style judge structurally rewards uniformity and cannot credit style-appropriate expressiveness. Also notable: the glasses-push quirk propagates through the anchor into BOTH arms — the anchor alone already carries her #1 identity marker.
Restated value proposition: the anchor is the consistency backbone (always pass it — or a fixed ref — for cohesion); the library buys expressive range in her voice — style-appropriate signature behavior at a small, measurable uniformity cost (−0.007 face, −0.010 voice pairwise). The channel choice is monotone-consistent vs expressive-consistent — a human call on the blind cuts above (which feels more like a real person's channel?), not one the automated judge can make.

Phase 6 (proposed) — the lineup test: is the expressive range hers, or just what Seedance does?

Everything above is one character. That is enough to establish that a library changes the output, and not enough to say the change is a personality. Every claim in the scorecard is equally consistent with a boring alternative: any character run through this pipeline gets hand-on-chest for empathetic and finger-counting for emphatic, because that is the engine's default gesture vocabulary for expressive footage — not Juno's. With n=1 the two hypotheses are indistinguishable. Phase 6 separates them by adding characters and asking whether they stay distinguishable when you take the face away.

part 1 · build

2–3 more characters, identical pipeline

  • New character bibles with non-overlapping signature quirks by construction — Juno's are glasses-push, lean-in, crooked smile, so the others must not reuse them.
  • gpt-image-2 identity photo → anchor → gated 8-emotion library at best-of-two, exactly as here. Any pipeline change confounds character with process.
  • At least one character adversarially similar to Juno — another woman, similar age, also glasses — so the lineup cannot be passed on face or wardrobe alone.
  • Measured cost: Juno's library took 16 Seedance generations (1 anchor + 15 emotion takes for 8 emotions, including re-rolls). Budget ~16–20 per character ⇒ ~48–60 clips for three.
part 2 · convergence

Do the libraries themselves collapse?

  • Mannerism fingerprinting: Gemini extracts a gesture/mannerism inventory per library, then measure overlap across characters. High cross-character overlap = the library is engine defaults, not character.
  • Voice check — the sleeper risk. Faces trivially differ (different photos; cross-character ArcFace is only a sanity control), but the voices are native Seedance audio and nothing forces them apart. If cross-character voice cosine is high, "a virtual voice can be locked without TTS" quietly becomes "all virtual characters get approximately the same voice" — which changes the multi-character value prop.

Part 3 · the lineup itself — the recognizability test proper

Render the same scripts across all characters (same beats, so content cannot give it away), then run blind attribution: the judge studies each character's library, receives shuffled new renders, and must say who is who. Two masking levels, because only one of them is the actual question.

ConditionWhat it isWhat it tells you
Unmaskedfull clip, face and audio intact CEILING / CONTROL Trivially easy. If attribution isn't near-perfect here, the judge protocol is broken and nothing else in the run is readable.
Identity-suppressedface blurred and/or audio muted THE REAL TEST Can the judge attribute above chance from body language alone? This is the only condition where a pass means the libraries encode distinct personalities.

outcome A

They stay distinguishable when masked

The library pipeline is a genuine personality mechanism, not just an expressiveness knob — and the multi-character virtual-cast story holds: you can build a cast whose members read as different people from body language alone.

outcome B

They converge

The honest restatement: the library buys expressiveness, not identity. Routing still ships for the authenticity win, but "distinct virtual characters" would need the differentiation injected somewhere else — prompt-level quirk conditioning per render, or engine-side.

⚠ not a blocker

Nothing in the shipping path waits on this. The 2-way router is cleared on Phase 3's evidence and should ship with a motion floor and an identity gate on the routed reference. Phase 6 decides how far the virtual-cast claim can be pushed, not whether routing works.

Phase 3 prompt sampling — Snowflake via Hex 
-- HEYGEN.HEYGEN_MYSQL.SEEDANCE_GENERATIONS (timestamp col = CREATED_TS)
WITH base AS (
  SELECT sg.PROMPT, sg.USERNAME, sg.RATIO, sg.CREATED_TS
  FROM HEYGEN.HEYGEN_MYSQL.SEEDANCE_GENERATIONS sg
  WHERE sg.GENERATION_STATUS = 'completed'
    AND sg.CREATED_TS >= DATEADD('day', -21, CURRENT_DATE())
    AND LENGTH(sg.PROMPT) BETWEEN 120 AND 900
    AND (sg.PROMPT LIKE '%"%"%' OR sg.PROMPT ILIKE '%she says%' OR sg.PROMPT ILIKE '%he says%'
         OR sg.PROMPT ILIKE '%saying "%' OR sg.PROMPT ILIKE '%dialogue%')
), dedup AS (
  SELECT PROMPT, MIN(USERNAME) AS username, COUNT(DISTINCT USERNAME) AS n_users,
         MAX(CREATED_TS) AS last_used, MAX(RATIO) AS ratio
  FROM base GROUP BY PROMPT
)
SELECT d.PROMPT, d.n_users, d.last_used, d.ratio
FROM dedup d JOIN DBT.PROD_INT.USER_CENTER uc ON uc.USERNAME = d.username
WHERE d.n_users < 5 AND uc.EMAIL NOT LIKE '%@heygen.com%' AND uc.EMAIL NOT LIKE '%@wenjun.com%'
ORDER BY d.last_used DESC LIMIT 60
-- 60 rows returned; 8 selected for scenario diversity; person descriptor adapted to
-- "the woman from the reference video", scene/wardrobe/action/dialogue kept verbatim.
Gate methodology — per-clip JSON in ~/virtual_twin_library/review/ 
identity : ArcFace (insightface buffalo_l) cosine, 6 evenly-spaced frames vs identity.png → mean/min
voice    : resemblyzer speaker-embedding cosine, clip audio (16k mono) vs anchor.wav
gesture  : Gemini 3.1 Pro (gemini-3.1-pro-preview, temp 0, response_schema-forced JSON) watches the mp4
           with the generation prompt; reports transcript, gesture seen, glasses, artifacts
script   : difflib ratio(normalized expected line, normalized transcript) — catches ref-reconstruction leak
pass     : arc_mean ≥ .45 ∧ artifacts ≤ minor ∧ gesture_match ∧ glasses ∧ script ≥ .75 ∧ voice ≥ .75
